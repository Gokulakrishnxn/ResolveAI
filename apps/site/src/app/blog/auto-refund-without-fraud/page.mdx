# How to auto-refund without giving away the store

**Posted March 5, 2026 — Dario L.**

Most merchants are afraid to turn on auto-refunds because they remember the one time someone bought 6 pairs of $80 sneakers and claimed they all arrived damaged.

The honest answer is: you only need three numbers and two rules to make auto-refunds safe.

## The three numbers

1. **Refunds in last 30 days, per customer.** If this is greater than 2, escalate.
2. **Refund value / lifetime value.** If &gt; 0.4, escalate.
3. **Dispute count.** If &gt; 0, escalate.

That&apos;s the entire fraud guard. We compute these in the background every hour and store them on the customer record.

## The two rules

1. **Cap by amount.** Auto-approve refunds up to $50 (or whatever your gross margin lets you absorb without thinking). Above that, draft + human approves.
2. **Require evidence for damage.** &quot;Wrong item&quot; and &quot;damaged&quot; require a photo. Other reason codes don&apos;t.

## The one flag

Add `manual_review` to a customer&apos;s flags any time you have a gut feeling. They&apos;re permanently routed to humans regardless of every rule above. Trust your operators — they&apos;re cheap insurance.

## What you&apos;ll see

In our data across ~200 stores running this configuration:

- 78% of refund requests are auto-approved.
- 0.3% of auto-approvals are reversed in retrospect.
- Net effect: ~3 hours/day of human time freed, refund cycle time drops to under 2 hours.

Auto-refunds are not a binary on/off. They&apos;re a thoughtfully-bounded automation, and the bounds are not that hard.
