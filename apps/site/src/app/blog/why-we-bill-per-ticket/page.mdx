# Why we bill per ticket, not per seat

**Posted April 12, 2026 — Maya P.**

Seat-based pricing is a fossil from the world where humans were the unit of work in customer support. The math used to be simple: more agents, bigger team, bigger SaaS bill.

That world is gone.

When most of your tickets are resolved by software in 2 seconds, charging by &quot;agent seat&quot; is a category error. It punishes the merchants who automate the most. It rewards us — the SaaS — when our customers add headcount, which is exactly the opposite of what we should incentivize.

So we picked the actual unit: **a ticket the system actually processed**.

## What that looks like in practice

- Starter: $29/month, 500 tickets included.
- Growth: $99/month, 2,500 tickets included.
- Scale: $299/month, 10,000 tickets included.
- Overage: $0.05/ticket, regardless of plan.

Every plan includes unlimited human seats. Add your VA. Add the founder. Add the night shift contractor. We don&apos;t care — they&apos;re free.

## Why it&apos;s good for you

If our AI is bad and you re-route every ticket to a human, you don&apos;t pay us extra — we &quot;processed&quot; the ticket but you didn&apos;t get value, and that&apos;s on us. If our AI is good and you fully automate 90% of the volume, you also don&apos;t pay us extra (you stay inside your bundle). The pricing breaks down only when you grow — which is what we both want.

## Why it&apos;s good for us

We can&apos;t hide a bad product behind seat upgrades. Either the AI works and the merchant stays, or it doesn&apos;t and they leave. There&apos;s nowhere to extract margin without showing the work.

We think this is what category-defining SaaS looks like in 2026: priced like infrastructure, not like seats.
